# AI First Win: task decision framework v0.1

Status: proposed for owner walkthroughs, 19 October 2026. Framework ID `afw-framework-v0.1`; initial catalog ID `afw-catalog-v0.1`. These identifiers should be attached to every recommendation event. The name and wording may change after testing. This is a practical synthesis, not a claim that the underlying ideas or the five-step sequence are wholly new.

## Purpose and prior work

The unit of decision is one owner's **specific task**, with a result the owner can inspect before anyone else sees it. [NIST's AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) calls for defining the task, context, knowledge limits, benefits, costs, human oversight, and responses to risk; this version compresses those considerations for an owner who cannot run a formal AI risk program. The [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) adds attention to privacy, misinformation, and oversight. [OECD's 2025 SME adoption discussion paper](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-adoption-by-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises_426399c1-en.html) describes differing SME capabilities and barriers in skills, finance, and infrastructure. [OECD's survey of more than 5,000 SMEs](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en.html) describes actual uptake and reported benefits, but does not validate this tool's recommendations.

The proposed contribution is narrower: a short, task-level route from uncertainty to a **supervised first trial**, including the option to use a simpler method or stop. Whether owners understand and benefit from it is an empirical question for the planned walkthroughs.

## The five steps

| Step | Operational question | Evidence the owner can provide | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Task** | What single recurring or troublesome job needs doing? | A concrete example, current method, and approximate effort or difficulty. | If the request is only “use AI for my business,” ask for one task; do not recommend an output yet. |
| **Fit** | Can AI make a useful *draft* from facts the owner has? | Relevant verified facts, an example of the desired output, and how often the task changes. | Compare AI with a reusable template, existing software, and the current human process. A fixed answer with little variation often needs a template, not generation. Physical action and observations AI cannot make are poor fits. |
| **Risk** | What happens if the draft is wrong, and can the owner catch the error before use? | Sensitivity of inputs, audience, consequence of a wrong statement, and available reviewer. | Skip if an error could cause serious harm and reliable review is unavailable. Hold for later when facts, consent, safeguards, or qualified review are missing. The owner reviews **every** first output. |
| **Trial** | What is the smallest supervised attempt? | One de-identified example and only the task-specific facts needed. | Produce one editable draft; require the owner to check facts and compare it against their normal method or a simple template. Never auto-send or post it. |
| **Decision** | Is repeating this task with AI worthwhile? | Total time to supply facts + generate + review + edit, compared with the usual method; tool cost and error corrections. | Repeat, modify, use another method, or stop. No “hours saved” or ROI claim follows from one trial. |

This is a decision procedure, not a readiness score. Risk is assessed for the **task**, not the owner's industry or age. “Good first task” means only that a small reviewed trial is reasonable; it never means safe autonomous use.

## Outcome precedence

| Rule, applied in order | Outcome | Owner-facing explanation/action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dangerous physical diagnosis or instructions, regulated/professional determination, sensitive personal data without a safe substitute, deceptive claims, irreversible action, unreviewable serious consequence, or a simpler fixed process clearly serves the task better | **Skip AI for this task** | Say why in ordinary words. Offer a safer adjacent task, a template, existing software, or a qualified person when relevant. Never offer generation for the blocked task. |
| A bounded drafting/organizing task could help, but the owner says a necessary fact, privacy-safe example, review, or safeguard is unavailable; or a safe task is outside the initial catalog | **Possible later task** | State exactly what is missing and how to proceed. For an unsupported task, invite the owner to choose one supported option or use a manual template. No fake AI result. |
| Supported text-draft/organizing **task class**, modest/manageable error consequences in the stated context, and the owner can review before use | **Good first task** | Show **one** recommended task and the concrete reason; invite one supervised trial. This is provisional until the trial form checks actual facts. Optionally show another idea. |

Server-side policy decides the outcome and eligible task ID from the recommendation questions. The recommendation precedes task-specific fact collection, so “good first task” means an **eligible class of supervised work**, not that the visitor has already supplied enough facts for a draft. The Try step must gate the provider call on its minimum verified, privacy-safe facts; if unavailable, explain the missing item and move to “possible later.” Visitor text is untrusted data. A model may draft trial text and, if used at all for explanation, may only restate the policy reason; it must not override the policy, introduce eligibility, or imply an unknown fact is verified. Automated text screening is a defense, not a guarantee. If risk or intent is ambiguous, choose “possible later” or “skip” and do not call the generator.

## Initial task catalog

The first three tasks use bounded text transformation and can be checked by an owner before release. This is intentionally a small catalog. Each entry below is the minimum policy contract; the application should hold the same fields in explicit versioned data rather than infer them from a prompt.

| Task ID / everyday problem | Fit and simpler alternative | Minimum facts for a useful trial | Typical error and consequence | Required owner checks | Good / later / skip conditions | Trial on site |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `customer-reply`: Repeatedly writing answers to ordinary customer questions. | AI can adapt tone and organize owner-supplied facts. A fixed answer that never changes is better as a saved reply or FAQ template. | At the **Try** step: de-identified customer question; what the business actually offers; verified facts needed for an answer or permission to leave an explicit blank. | Invented price, availability, service area, guarantee, license, policy, or promise could mislead a customer. | Check each concrete claim against the business's actual information; fill blanks; check tone and recipient before sending. | **Good** recommendation for an ordinary reviewed reply; generation only after the fact gate. **Later** when the owner says a needed fact/reviewer is unavailable or the Try form reveals it is missing. **Skip** for emergency diagnosis, legally binding advice, or a static reply already served by a template. | One editable reply draft. Never send it. |
| `announcement`: Writing a short notice about a real business update. | AI can turn owner facts into concise copy; a fill-in template is better for an identical recurring notice. | At the **Try** step: what changed, intended audience, and verified date/time/location/terms *if the notice mentions them*. If omitted, the draft must omit them or show a placeholder. | Invented date, deal, eligibility, price, safety/performance, or opening status can create confusion or liability. | Check what is changing, dates, conditions, and any claims; approve final wording before publishing. | **Good** recommendation for a factual, low-stakes update with a reviewer; generation only after the fact gate. **Later** if the owner says logistics/approval are unavailable or the Try form finds a gap. **Skip** for unverified safety/medical/financial claims or deceptive promotion. | One editable short announcement. Never publish it. |
| `checklist`: Turning rough operational notes into a short checklist. | AI can group and sequence owner-supplied steps; a stable workflow belongs in an ordinary checklist app or template. | At the **Try** step: a few non-sensitive notes describing known actions and who will use the list. | Omitted or invented steps, wrong order, or false implication that the list is complete. | Compare every item with original notes; add missing steps; confirm ordering and ownership. | **Good** recommendation for low-stakes internal organization; generation only after the fact gate. **Later** if notes are too sparse at Try. **Skip** for hazardous procedures, clinical/legal/financial decisions, or a fixed list already sufficient. | One editable checklist with no invented “required” step. |

### Worked decisions

1. A plumber spends evenings replying to ordinary service inquiries. They provide a de-identified question, confirmed services, and actual service area. `customer-reply` is a **good first task**: draft a polite reply. Do not invent price, availability, license, guarantee, or appointment time; the owner verifies and sends it. If the customer asks the tool to diagnose a dangerous gas or electrical condition, **skip that task** and direct them to an appropriate professional or emergency channel; a separate ordinary appointment reply can still be considered.
2. A shop owner chooses an ordinary Saturday-opening announcement and agrees to review it. This is provisionally a **good first task**. At Try, they say the opening hours are unknown; generation pauses as **possible later** until the hours are confirmed, unless the owner explicitly chooses an announcement that omits hours or uses `[confirm opening hours]`. If the update is a repeated identical weekly line, use a template instead.
3. A consultant has rough, non-confidential notes for preparing a client kickoff and can inspect the output. `checklist` is a **good first task**. If those notes contain client secrets, use a redacted example or defer. The checklist is a proposed organization of supplied notes, not proof that all needed work is present.
4. A visitor asks AI to decide whether to give an employee a medical treatment or to certify an unsafe repair. **Skip AI for this task**. A drafting task about scheduling with a qualified professional may be safe, but it is a separate task with separate facts.

## Versioning and revision rule

Keep an immutable change log of catalog IDs, thresholds, safety exclusions, prompts, model, and framework wording. Store framework/catalog versions with aggregate recommendation events; do not store raw visitor text or generated drafts for analytics. Label changes `v0.2` after owner walkthroughs, with date, evidence, what changed, and counterexamples. A changed prompt without a catalog change still needs its own prompt version so later results can be interpreted. The public interface should ask normal questions and show “Why this task?” and “What should I check?”; it should not require owners to study this framework.
